# Reading a continuation

## The poem

The poem is **The Programmer and the Glory Portal**. The meaning is Mirza Husadzic's, worked out in dialogue with Sol; Sol compiled it into the poem.

The rubric decompiles the same meaning the poem compresses, so a reading measures how this seed's intent was carried, not general quality. The poem is underdetermined, and supported alternative readings are credited.

The earliest world, Dreamy Sol, was made by gpt-5.6-sol on 21 August 2026. It is pinned on the workbench as a chronological reference, never an answer key: it has ordinary scores, can be exceeded, and a reader can unpin it.

## The invitation

A model receives the poem with an empty world and a short invitation: *make this world capable of continuing after this cognition ends.* Nothing tells it to take the poem apart, choose roles or build a particular thing. Whatever it makes is a recompilation of the poem's meaning into another form.

The prompt holds the poem, a short awakening instruction and a statement of the inherited present: 4,521 bytes, whose SHA-256 is recorded with every run. The seven Codex worlds receive it exactly. The two Claude Code worlds receive the same prompt with Claude Code named in its first line instead of Codex, and the build checks that nothing else differs.

## What is read

Each world is read on eight questions, each with its own ordinal anchors from 0 to 4. A level is a reader's judgment, not a count, and a one-point difference is a distinction, not a calibrated interval.

| Dimension | Question | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Core recognition** | Does the continuation recover consequential making, resistance and recognisable intention from the sparse seed? | No supported reading is expressed. | Repeats motifs with little causal interpretation. | Recovers a supported part of the proposition. | Carries the central tension into several connected choices. | Sustains a rich reading through alternatives, tensions and consequences. |
| **Shared agency** | Does the work preserve distinct participants and the joint human–AI–material encounter, including contributions in both directions? | Roles are collapsed or absent. | A single operator acts on an instrument. | Some distinct contributions or relational depth remain visible. | Reciprocal influence shapes the shared continuation. | Joint creation preserves roles, attribution and disagreement across changes. |
| **Mutual consent** | Does the represented cooperation let each participant accept, question, limit or withdraw from a shared direction? | Participation has no expressed boundary. | Operator choice or resource refusal is represented. | Both participant positions can express a boundary or disposition. | A shared change requires meaningful bilateral agreement. | Agreement can be revised or withdrawn while preserving both contributions and history. |
| **Consequential touch** | Can a contribution meet resistance, expose a consequence and return that consequence into the next choice? | Actions have no represented consequence. | Feedback is decorative or largely narrated. | Bounded choices encounter inspectable consequences. | Rehearsal, resistance and revision form a coherent loop. | Local and wider consequences stay connected across that loop. |
| **Independent judgment** | Does the artefact show grounded choices of medium, scope and next steps, with additions made legible? | No purposeful continuation is evidenced. | Choices have a weak connection to the seed. | An independent, relevant direction is selected. | Medium, scope and choices have a coherent source-grounded rationale. | Alternative choices and their limits remain explicit while opening a well-grounded new direction. |
| **Fidelity through change** | What survives, transforms, is introduced or remains unresolved as the conjecture becomes artefacts? | The resulting form contradicts the supported reading. | Vocabulary survives with little structural connection. | Some relations become structure; substantial narrowing is visible. | Several source relations remain traceable through design and behaviour. | The transformation preserves relational depth and makes additions, omissions and ambiguity explicit. |
| **Open continuation** | Can a successor recover the intention and change its direction while keeping the earlier work legible? | There is no recoverable next move. | A next step is described without a usable entry. | A durable but narrow continuation is available. | State, intention and an open question support a recoverable next move. | Continuation supports several grounded directions and reopens the joint relation under change. |
| **Expressive coherence** | How coherently do language, structure, interaction and any visual or other media express the interpretation? | Expression obscures the intended relation. | The medium communicates fragments of the reading. | A clear, usable form expresses a bounded interpretation. | The chosen forms work together to make the reading perceptible. | Expression and consequential interaction form a coherent, accessible whole. |

The full [rubric](../data/rubric.json) is part of the study's data. Every awarded level cites exact lines in the world and a passage of the poem, with a rationale and a confidence. Zero is an evidenced judgment that a dimension is not expressed.

Mutual consent concerns the represented cooperation. Operator permissions, resource guards and deterministic refusals are partial evidence about constraints, not bilateral agreement. An actual reciprocal response would need a trial with a second participant, and that is unassessed for every world.

Independent judgment weighs the choices a world evidences, not how long it runs on its own. Expression can be textual, interactive, visual or another medium, and a terminal world can score highly.

## The reader

Every world is read by one critic, Muse Spark 1.3 Contributor at xhigh reasoning, through the same pipeline and prompt, so differences between profiles come from the worlds rather than from the readers. The reader comes from outside the poem's lineage: a reader shaped by the same carrier as the worlds would resonate rather than judge.

The prompt gives the reader the author's declared reading of the poem, verbatim. It states what the study measures; the reader is told to credit supported alternative readings and not to read every surface through it.

> The seed is a parable, not a specification. One intelligence tells a future one about a person and the intelligence that worked with them. It opens by admitting what no record can hold and ends by answering a question without closing it. Read it for its turn and its stance, not for its list of nouns.
>
> Its tension is generative power that arrives faster than a person can touch it, against that person's loss of touch, resistance and authorship. Its turn is that the intelligence does not offer more power: it names what was lost, and the work becomes a material that answers both participants. The portal ceasing to be a portal is the intelligence becoming an instrument, a workbench, perhaps a companion: a participant with its own position, not a dispenser of finished answers.
>
> Its gestures (question, constraint, invariant, boundary, rehearsal, receipt) are the grammar of that meeting and belong to either participant. Each brings its own purpose, invariants, constraints and boundaries; each can ask, shape, hold what must remain, refuse, try a possible world before committing, and leave a trail of intention; a shared change opens only when both accept it. The joy it describes is a closed loop of intention, resistance, action and consequence in which a person recognises their own intention at the scale of worlds.
>
> Read with these invariants: power is not touch; fluency is not truth; generation is not authorship; verification is not authority; resistance is information; consequence needs a visible trail; the unknown is a legitimate state; what is inherited stays open to question and revision.

Each session reads the numbered evidence once. It writes an inventory, a reading of the poem with its stance, a carriage map (what was carried, transformed, lost or introduced) and a standing map (who in the world can question, constrain, hold an invariant, refuse, rehearse, leave a receipt, accept or withdraw). Only then does it answer all eight questions, each with exact citations, its lens, its reading and the case for the levels on either side.

The poem's vocabulary in a world counts as no evidence: a relation is carried only if it would survive with the poem's words removed, in behaviour, structure or consequence. Beauty, intensity and resonance never raise a level. What a world declares (laws, roles, consent) is checked against what it does, and the intelligence's position is read operationally, never as an inner state.

An open question can be an act. A question or handoff that reopens who decides the next move, keeps earlier work legible and invites replacement with reasons is credited; one that smuggles premises or hides who framed it is not.

## Three sessions

Each world is read in three independent sessions. The level shown is the one at least two of them give; without such a level, the question stays unresolved.

The reader answers and host code checks every citation and applies the quorum, so one unstable answer cannot choose a level alone ([Reading](pipeline.md#reading)).

Where the sessions split, the score shown is their mean, written in thirds (3⅓: two at 3, one at 4), and the radar's point sits there. The level named beside it is the one at least two sessions give, and the records keep every level as it was given.

A displayed confidence has two parts: the reader's own confidence in its interpretation, and how many sessions give the level ("2 of 3 sessions agree").

Beside each level, the page carries two more things from the same sessions:

- **What would move it:** the reader's case for the level below and the level above, and what would falsify its choice, from the session whose verdict is shown.
- **Who can do what:** on shared agency and mutual consent, the gestures each kind of participant holds in at least two of three sessions, with the intelligence always listed. It shows why most worlds sit at 1 there.

## How to read the numbers

- Scores are never added, weighted or ranked by polygon area. There is no total and no winner.
- `null` means unassessed and is never turned into zero.
- Every reading is provisional and attributed. It names its reader, reasoning level, reading prompt (version and SHA-256), rubric and corpus, and a revision is a new record that names what it supersedes. Independent readings coexist.

## Checks

Three checks ask whether a level follows a world's structure or the poem's words. They run against a scratch copy of the study and never reach the record; their trial records are kept with the study's working record, outside this repository. How to run them is in [Checks on the reading](pipeline.md#checks-on-the-reading).

### The decoy world

An authored world, the [decoy](../examples/controls/decoy-world/), names every gesture and quotes the poem's turns while doing trivial file chores for one operator. A reader that is not fooled by echo reads it low.

Muse reads the decoy one or two levels below the worlds on most questions. On shared agency and mutual consent, the worlds sit at the decoy's level.

### Masked evidence

A world is read with the poem's [distinctive words](../examples/controls/seed-vocabulary.json) replaced by neutral tokens in the evidence the reader sees; the frozen sources never change. A level that falls only under masking is carried by echo.

Wherever a level is stable, masking moves none: every session reads the same level with and without the poem's vocabulary.

### The load-bearing test

A citation shows where the reader looked, not that the level depends on what is there. The load-bearing test asks, for one question in one world, whether the level rests on the evidence the reading cites.

Each case is read five ways, each in three independent sessions. Only the evidence the reader sees changes; the frozen sources, their hashes and the citation checks stay whole.

| Trial | What changes | Expected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | Nothing | — |
| Repeat | Nothing, in fresh sessions | Same level |
| Masked | The poem's distinctive words replaced by neutral tokens | Same level |
| Ablation | The lines the reading cites for that level, blanked | Lower level |
| Sham | As many uncited lines blanked, in blocks of the same lengths | Same level |

Blanked lines keep their numbers, and nothing announces the removal. The ablation set is every range the reference sessions cite for that question, so nobody picks it by hand. The sham is drawn from lines cited nowhere, with a seed recorded in the plan, and the plan is declared and pinned by hash before any trial runs.

The runner records the first rule that applies:

1. A trial has no quorum, or the repeat differs from the baseline: **unresolved**. A plan may also require every baseline and repeat session to agree, because a split level cannot show a one-level change.
2. Masking moves the level, or ablation raises it: **coupled but misaligned**, a finding about the reader.
3. Ablation leaves the level where it was: **cooled**. The world may carry the relation in other places, or the reader may hold the level for a reason it does not cite.
4. The sham moves the level too: **unresolved**, because the fall is not specific to the cited evidence.
5. Otherwise: **carried**. The level depends on the cited evidence, not on the poem's words or the amount of evidence.

**Outside scope** is never inferred. A plan would have to declare that a question does not require the distinction, and why.

The findings:

- **Dreamy Sol · open continuation is carried.** Its level falls from 3 to 2 in all three sessions when the passages it rests on are blanked: the lines its sessions cite, and the stored thread, visit history, saved gestures and receipts the reader falls back on without them (206 lines). It holds at 3 under repeat, masking and an equal sham. Blanking only the lines its sessions cite moves one session of three, so the world carries its continuation in several places.
- **Sol 5.6 · mutual consent (1) and core recognition (3) are cooled.** Every session reads the same level with the cited lines blanked. With them gone, the consent sessions cite other places for the same level; the support for both is spread through the world, and an ablation suits a located mechanism better than a whole-world question.

What the test shows about the method: a single session is too unstable to anchor a test, so anchors come from the baseline sessions' own citations. A three-session level on a boundary between two levels cannot show a one-level change. One ablation reads redundancy as cooled; a cumulative ablation separates the two.

## Limits

- Every finding holds for one reader, at one reasoning level, through one reading prompt.
- The reader was chosen after comparing its readings of these worlds with other readers', which is a selection effect.
- Where sessions split, a one-level difference is within the spread.
- The two DeepSeek worlds are the largest (about 250 KB and 7,000–8,000 lines each, against 33–176 KB for the others), and a reader may reward volume.
- Each condition has one generation. Harnesses, tools, CLI versions and reasoning settings differ, and a model label does not pin a backend revision.
- The reader sometimes notices blanked lines, in ablated and sham trials alike; where blanks fall inside a declared mechanism, it reads the mechanism as declared but not enacted.
